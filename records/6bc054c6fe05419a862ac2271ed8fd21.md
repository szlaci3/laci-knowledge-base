# Security as a Conscious Design Decision

Security should become a first-class consideration in how I build and use agentic software.

Until now, I have often optimized primarily for capability and convenience without explicitly evaluating the security implications. For example, I usually choose **Codex mode** rather than the safer **codex-in-sbx** environment because Codex mode gives me important practical advantages: remote control from my phone, a better UI, easier access to reopened conversations, built-in voice features, and a generally smoother workflow.

The goal is **not to always choose the safest possible environment**. Excessive isolation can make tools significantly less useful. The goal is to make the trade-off consciously rather than implicitly.

From now on, when adopting or designing agentic software, I should consider:

- What access does the agent have to my machine, files, credentials, network, and external services?
- What can it execute or modify without additional approval?
- What could happen if the model makes a mistake, follows malicious content, or receives a prompt injection?
- Can permissions or capabilities be reduced without significantly damaging the workflow?
- When I choose a less-isolated environment for convenience, what additional risk am I accepting and is that trade-off justified?

Prefer **least privilege and isolation by default where practical**, but allow broader capabilities when they provide meaningful benefits and the risks are understood.

This applies both to **software and tools I already use** and to **everything I build or adopt in the future**.
